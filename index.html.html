Biography & Background

Professional Biography

"Bridging analytical finance, modern information systems, and academic practice to understand how organizations adapt to technological transformation."

I am a Business Researcher and Graduate Teaching Assistant at BRAC Business School, BRAC University, Bangladesh. My academic journey began with a Bachelor of Business Administration (BBA) majoring in Finance and minoring in Computer Information Management (CIM) at BRAC University.

During my undergraduate studies, I developed a keen interest in the convergence of quantitative financial methods and information management systems. This dual specialization sparked my curiosity about how digital infrastructure and data analytics reshape strategic decision-making in corporate and logistical environments.

Prior to joining BRAC Business School as a GTA, I gained practical corporate insight as an intern in the Credit Risk Management division at United Commercial Bank PLC (UCB). Analyzing credit appraisals and corporate balance sheets exposed me to the realities of institutional financial risk—further motivating my desire to pursue empirical research at the intersection of business and technology.

As a Graduate Teaching Assistant, I support undergraduate learning, mentor students in business fundamentals, and collaborate with faculty on active research projects. My current research focuses on artificial intelligence application in supply chains, urban last-mile delivery challenges, dynamic logistics routing, and technology-enhanced higher education.

AI-Driven Last-Mile Delivery Case Detail

Research Question: How can machine learning models incorporate real-time informal traffic metrics in dense megacities where standard dynamic routing algorithms fail?

Methodology: Aggregated secondary logistics time-stamps and local traffic variance data across key delivery hubs in Dhaka. Simulated adaptive route selection against static shortest-path baselines.

Key Findings: Factoring localized bottlenecks and street-width constraints reduced estimated delivery delay by up to 22% compared to standard GPS models.

Why Local Context Matters in AI-Powered Last-Mile Delivery

When software engineers build routing algorithms in Silicon Valley or Western European innovation hubs, they implicitly rely on structured infrastructure: digitized street grids, predictable speed limits, reliable lane divisions, and automated traffic telemetry.

However, deploying these exact models in high-density South Asian cities such as Dhaka reveals significant friction. Informal transport modes, non-standard street widths, dynamic local market setups, and micro-weather patterns break standard shortest-path algorithms.

True optimization requires AI systems designed with hyper-local context—incorporating non-traditional data streams such as community-reported transit conditions and localized time-window heuristics.

Finance Students in an Increasingly Digital Business World

Historically, financial education emphasized statement analysis, time value of money, and corporate valuation. While these quantitative foundations remain indispensable, modern financial institutions are fundamentally software-driven organizations.

Today’s finance graduate must possess an understanding of management information systems, database query structures, automated credit scoring models, and basic programming concepts.
